The business problem

Summit Event Management Group (SEMG) is the event management scenario used in this academic project. Its operations cover conferences, workshops and private celebrations.

The brief described a spreadsheet-based process that had become difficult to manage as events, clients, services and payments grew. The goal was to organise these records in a connected database that supports reporting.

My contribution

Completed for MBIS623 Data Management at the University of Canterbury. The original ER diagram was supplied in the assignment.

  • Implemented the relational model in SQLite.
  • Added keys, relationship tables and data constraints.
  • Extended the model for speaker bookings and payments.
  • Wrote and interpreted two financial reporting queries.

How the data is organised

Primary and foreign keys connect the business entities. Associative tables represent many-to-many relationships, including staff assignments, event services and workshop speakers.

Event operations

Clients, venues and events, supported by staff assignments and event services.

Participation

Attendees and tickets, with workshops, speakers and areas of expertise.

Financial records

Client invoices and payments, plus speaker bookings, invoices and payments.

Extending the model

The original model linked speakers to workshops. I added a separate structure to track their event bookings and financial obligations.

Speaker_Booking records the event, speaker, fee and contract status. Speaker_Invoice stores the related invoice. Speaker_Payment records payments against that invoice.

Data integrity

The schema uses primary keys, foreign keys, composite keys and NOT NULL, UNIQUE and CHECK constraints. For example, monetary values cannot be negative and service quantities must be positive.

The uploaded database passed SQLite's integrity check and foreign-key check, with no foreign-key violations found. Foreign-key enforcement must also be enabled for each connection.

Two questions answered with SQL

01 / PAYMENT TRACKING

Which client invoices have not been fully paid?

The query joins clients, events, invoices and payments, then subtracts the total paid from each invoice amount. A LEFT JOIN keeps invoices even when no payment exists, while COALESCE treats a missing payment total as zero.

InvoiceClientInvoiced PaidOutstanding
1014Digital NZ$10,500 $9,500$1,000
1015Digital NZ$2,500 $1,500$1,000

Both invoices relate to the Multi-Day Digital Business Conference. The report highlights $2,000 in outstanding balances for payment follow-up.

Explore the SQL logic 
Invoice.total_amount
  - COALESCE(SUM(Payment.amount_paid), 0)
  AS outstanding_amount

-- Group payments by invoice.
-- Keep only invoices with an unpaid balance.
HAVING outstanding_amount > 0
ORDER BY outstanding_amount DESC;

02 / FINANCIAL REPORTING

Which event has the lowest financial result?

This query compares invoiced revenue with quoted service costs and booked speaker fees. Each source is aggregated by event before joining, avoiding double counting across multiple records.

Lowest result: Natural Beauty Product Launch

$3,200Invoiced revenue
$3,350Quoted service costs
$4,000Booked speaker fees
−$4,150Revenue less selected costs

Selected costs exceed invoiced revenue in this sample event, flagging it for a review of pricing and budgeting.

Explore the SQL logic 
-- Calculate separate totals at event level.
SELECT event_id, SUM(total_amount)
FROM Invoice
GROUP BY event_id;

SELECT event_id, SUM(quoted_cost)
FROM Event_Service
GROUP BY event_id;

SELECT event_id, SUM(booking_fee)
FROM Speaker_Booking
GROUP BY event_id;

-- Join these totals to Event, then calculate:
-- invoiced revenue - service costs - speaker fees

Illustration of the aggregation approach; the complete query is on GitHub.

Results come from the project's sample data. The financial measure includes selected costs only: it is not complete accounting profit or cash received.

What I learned

This project connected relational database implementation with business reporting. It reinforced the importance of choosing the correct aggregation level, handling missing payments and defining financial measures precisely.

Scope & next steps

This is an academic demonstration rather than a production system. Future improvements could include broader operating costs, overdue-payment reporting and additional validation of dates and booking statuses.
