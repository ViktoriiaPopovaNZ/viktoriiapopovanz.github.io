Research question and data

Which customer and booking characteristics predict hotel booking cancellations?

The Hotel Booking Demand dataset contains 119,390 bookings and 32 variables from a city hotel and a resort hotel in Portugal, covering arrivals between 2015 and 2017. The outcome is whether a booking was cancelled.

The analysis considered lead time, deposit type, market segment, previous cancellations, special requests and hotel type.

Data source: Antonio, Almeida & Nunes (2019), Hotel booking demand datasets, Data in Brief, 22, 41–49.

Exploratory findings

Longer lead time was associated with more cancellations

Cancellation rates increased from 18.6% for bookings made within 30 days of arrival to 67.7% for bookings made more than 365 days ahead. Median lead time was 113 days for cancelled bookings and 45 days for non-cancelled bookings.

These percentages describe each deposit category separately. The Refundable category contained relatively few bookings.

Deposit categories differed in other characteristics

Among Non Refund bookings, 97.2% came from the Groups and Offline TA/TO market segments. Their median lead time was 183 days, compared with 56 days for No Deposit bookings. Previous cancellation history was also more common: 25.0% versus 2.7%.

These differences motivated a model that considered several predictors together rather than interpreting deposit type in isolation.

Logistic regression

A multiple logistic regression model considered lead time, deposit type, market segment, previous cancellation history, special requests and hotel type together.

Non Refund bookings had substantially higher adjusted odds of cancellation than No Deposit bookings (OR 377.27). Previous cancellation history was also strongly associated with cancellation (OR 21.57).

Each additional 30 days of lead time was associated with approximately 13% higher odds. Each additional special request was associated with approximately 52% lower odds.

Odds ratios describe associations within this model. They are not probability ratios and do not establish causation.

Model evaluation

Accuracy did not tell the whole story

At a classification threshold of 0.5, the fitted model had 79.2% accuracy, 52.6% sensitivity and 94.9% specificity on the fitting data. It identified only about half of actual cancellations despite its relatively high overall accuracy.

Comparing thresholds highlighted the trade-off between missed cancellations and false alarms.

Conclusions and limitations

Lead time, deposit type, previous cancellations and special requests were associated with booking cancellation. Logistic regression provided an interpretable baseline, while threshold analysis showed why accuracy alone was insufficient for evaluating cancellation detection.

The extreme Non Refund association requires further investigation into the meaning and recording of this variable. The findings are specific to this dataset and should not be interpreted as evidence that a deposit policy causes cancellations.

Further work could investigate additional predictors and interactions, validate the model using a time-based split, and evaluate performance on data from other hotels.
